Where Does India's Electricity Come From?

A deep dive into India's power generation mix, grid emissions, and what it means for your EV's true well-to-wheel environmental impact.

7 MIN READ EV CAR WALE TEAM UPDATED JULY 2026

Every time you plug in your electric vehicle, you draw power from India's national electricity grid — a vast network of thermal power plants, hydroelectric dams, solar parks, and wind farms. But where does that power originate, and how green is it really? Understanding India's evolving energy mix reveals the true well-to-wheel carbon reduction achieved when switching from petrol or diesel to electricity.

01

India's Electricity Generation Mix

🔌 Clean Energy Grid Progress

Even when charged from a grid relying on thermal power, EVs produce 40% to 50% fewer lifecycle CO₂ emissions than petrol or diesel cars. As India targets 50% non-fossil capacity by 2030, your EV becomes cleaner every year.

India is the third-largest electricity producer in the world. As of 2026, the country's installed capacity has surpassed 430 Gigawatts (GW). While thermal energy still forms the baseline of the national grid, renewable sources are expanding at an unprecedented rate.

India ranks 5th globally in installed solar capacity and is steadily transitioning toward 500 GW of clean energy by 2030.

Coal's share in power generation has declined from 65% in 2020 to 55% in 2026. Meanwhile, solar power has surpassed hydro to become India's fastest-growing electricity source, with major ultra-mega solar parks operating across Rajasthan, Gujarat, and Karnataka.

02

Grid Emission Factors & Well-to-Wheel Analysis

The Grid Emission Factor (GEF) measures the carbon intensity of electricity consumed from the grid. In India, the average grid emission factor stands at approximately 650 gCO₂/kWh.

Even with this coal-heavy grid, an EV driving 300 km consumes ~40 kWh of power, producing approximately 26 kg of CO₂. A comparable petrol car achieving 18 km/L over 300 km consumes 16.6 litres of fuel, emitting over 52 kg of direct and indirect CO₂ — proving that EVs are twice as clean even on today's grid.

How EV Charging Impacts the Grid

A standard 7.2 kW home EV charger consumes roughly the same instantaneous power as running 5 to 6 air conditioners simultaneously. Uncontrolled charging during peak evening demand hours (6 PM – 10 PM) can strain local transformers.

However, smart charging infrastructure and Time-of-Day (ToD) tariffs shift charging demand to overnight hours (11 PM – 6 AM). This utilizes idle thermal and wind power capacity, balancing the grid while offering EV owners discounted tariff rates.

⚡ Vehicle-to-Grid (V2G) Integration

V2G technology transforms EVs from power consumers into distributed battery storage assets. During peak grid stress, plugged-in EVs can safely discharge stored power back into the grid to stabilize local supply, earning revenue for vehicle owners while preventing power shortages.

04

Renewable Energy Certificates & Green Charging

Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs) allow charge point operators to buy green power credits. Major CPOs like Tata Power EZ Charge and ChargeZone are deploying 100% green-indexed highway charging hubs, ensuring every kWh delivered originates from solar or wind generators.

Time-of-Day (ToD) Tariffs for EV Owners

DISCOMs across Maharashtra, Delhi, Gujarat, and Karnataka offer specialized EV charging tariffs structured around grid demand periods:

  • • Night Off-Peak (11 PM – 6 AM): ₹4.0–₹5.5 / unit — Lowest rates for overnight scheduled home charging.
  • • Daytime Normal (6 AM – 6 PM): ₹6.5–₹8.0 / unit — Standard domestic / EV tariff rate.
  • • Evening Peak (6 PM – 11 PM): ₹9.0–₹12.0 / unit — Higher tariff rate to discourage grid congestion.
06

Rooftop Solar & EV Synergy

A 5 kW rooftop solar setup produces 20–25 units of clean power daily. Under the central government's PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana (providing subsidies up to ₹78,000), Indian homeowners can charge their EVs at near-zero operating cost while exporting excess solar energy back to the DISCOM via net metering.

Zero-Fuel Cost Mobility

Pairing a 30–40 kWh EV with a 5 kW rooftop solar array saves an average driver ₹65,000–₹90,000 annually in fuel costs, achieving complete financial payback within 3 to 4 years.

  • ✔ Rapid Decarbonization: India's grid is aggressively integrating solar and wind power, reducing thermal dependence every year.

Conclusion

Electricity powers the future of transportation, and understanding where it originates is key to evaluating the environmental impact of electric vehicles. While thermal power remains part of India's current baseline, the rapid expansion of solar and wind capacity ensures that every EV sold in India will become cleaner every single year of its operational lifespan.

By taking advantage of off-peak Time-of-Day tariffs, installing rooftop solar, or utilizing green-certified public fast chargers, Indian EV owners can maximize both their financial savings and environmental benefits.
