Companies Building India's EV Network

A deep dive into the key players powering India's electric vehicle charging infrastructure.

7 min read EV Car Wale Team Updated July 2026

India's EV revolution depends as much on charging infrastructure as it does on the vehicles themselves. Over the past three years, a mix of legacy energy companies, aggressive startups, and automotive OEMs have been racing to build out the country's charging network. As of mid-2026, India has crossed 25,000 public charging stations — up from just 1,800 in 2022. Here are the companies leading that charge.

Tata Power — The Incumbent Giant

Tata Power has emerged as India's largest EV charging network operator, leveraging its existing utility infrastructure and real estate partnerships. With over 5,000 public charging points across 450+ cities, Tata Power's EZ Charge network covers highways, retail outlets, residential societies, and commercial fleets.

Their strategy combines three key approaches: deploying 60 kW and 120 kW DC fast chargers along national highways, installing 7.2 kW AC chargers at apartments and offices, and forming exclusive partnerships with Tata Motors — every new Tata EV owner gets EZ Charge credits worth ₹10,000 included with purchase.

Tata Power has also partnered with Indian Oil and HPCL to co-locate chargers at existing fuel stations, a model that dramatically reduces land acquisition costs. Their pricing averages ₹12–18/kWh across DC chargers, making them competitive for highway charging.

ChargeZone — Highway Dominance

Founded in 2019, ChargeZone has strategically focused on intercity highway corridors, making them the go-to network for long-distance EV travel. They currently operate 1,200+ charging stations across 20+ states, with a strong presence on the Delhi–Mumbai, Mumbai–Pune, and Bengaluru–Chennai highway routes.

ChargeZone's chargers are predominantly 60 kW and 150 kW DC fast chargers with CCS2 connectors. Key highlights:

  • • Exclusive partnership with MG Motor — MG ZS EV owners get preferential pricing and reservation slots.
  • • Solar-integrated charging hubs at select locations reducing grid dependency.
  • • Pricing at ₹14–20/kWh depending on location and speed.
  • • 99.5% uptime guarantee with 24/7 remote monitoring at their Noida command centre.

Statiq — Network Aggregator

Statiq has taken a unique approach: rather than building all its own chargers, it operates an open network model that aggregates third-party chargers alongside its own proprietary stations. This allows them to claim 3,000+ touchpoints across India, making their app one of the most comprehensive in terms of charger discovery.

Their proprietary stations feature 30 kW and 60 kW DC chargers, while their network includes AC chargers from hotels, malls, and office complexes that have opened their private infrastructure to the public. Statiq's subscription model — ₹499/month for unlimited charging at select stations — has been a game-changer for fleet operators and daily commuters.

The company recently raised ₹200 crore in Series B funding to expand its proprietary charging corridor along the Golden Quadrilateral, targeting a charger every 50 km on all four arms by 2027.

Ather Grid — Two-Wheeler Powerhouse

Ather Energy's charging network, Ather Grid, is purpose-built for electric two-wheelers. With 800+ fast-charging points across 90+ cities, Ather Grid provides 60 kW DC charging that can take an Ather 450X from 0–80% in just 35 minutes. While primarily designed for Ather owners, the network is open to all CCS2-compatible two-wheelers and even some four-wheelers.

A unique feature: Ather Grid chargers are free for Ather owners for the first year, with a nominal ₹20/kWh after that. The company also offers a home charger installation service, creating an integrated charging ecosystem from home to highway. Their charger density in tier-1 cities like Bengaluru, Chennai, and Delhi is among the best in the country.

Zeon & Electric Pe — Regional Champions

Zeon has established a stronghold in South India with 600+ stations across Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Kerala, and Andhra Pradesh. Their strategy involves partnering with local malls, theatres, and restaurant chains to host chargers, making charging a natural part of daily routines. Pricing at ₹10–15/kWh is among the most affordable in the country.

Electric Pe, meanwhile, has focused intensely on Delhi-NCR and Mumbai with 400+ high-speed DC stations. They differentiate with 120 kW ultra-fast chargers that support vehicle-to-grid (V2G) capability, positioning themselves as grid-interactive infrastructure providers rather than just charging stations.

How Charging Networks Make Money

Most EV charging networks in India are still pre-profit, but the revenue model is becoming clearer:

  • • Margins on electricity: The primary revenue stream — networks buy power at industrial rates (₹6–8/kWh) and sell at ₹12–22/kWh, pocketing ₹6–10/kWh.
  • • Subscription fees: Monthly/annual plans (₹299–₹999/month) for unlimited or discounted charging.
  • • Advertising & brand partnerships: Charger screens and app placements generate ad revenue from automotive and lifestyle brands.
  • • Carbon credits: Networks earn carbon credits for enabling EV adoption, which can be sold on compliance markets.
  • • Real estate value: Charging stations increase foot traffic to host locations (malls, cafes), allowing networks to negotiate lower rent or revenue-sharing deals.

Industry estimates suggest a DC fast charger needs 30–40% utilisation to break even. Most Indian networks currently average 15–20%, but this is improving rapidly as EV adoption scales.

BP Pulse & Fortum — Global Players, Local Play

British energy giant BP entered the Indian EV charging market through its BP Pulse brand, which acquired a majority stake in Jio-BP's charging network. With 350+ stations primarily located at existing fuel pumps, BP Pulse benefits from high-visibility locations and established customer traffic. Their 120 kW ultra-fast chargers on the Delhi–Jaipur and Mumbai–Surat corridors are among the fastest available.

Fortum, the Finnish energy company, operates 200+ chargers in India under its Charge & Drive brand. While smaller in scale, Fortum has carved a niche in workplace charging — they've installed chargers at 50+ corporate campuses in Bengaluru and Gurugram, offering employees subsidised charging as a workplace benefit.

PlugShare — The Community Aggregator

PlugShare isn't a charging network operator — it's the Google Maps of EV charging. The app aggregates data from all major Indian networks plus crowd-sourced information from private charger owners who open their home chargers to the public. With over 15,000 listed locations across India, PlugShare is often the first app EV owners install.

Key features include real-time availability status, user reviews with photos, pricing transparency, and trip planning with charging stops. PlugShare has become essential for intercity travel, helping drivers identify which chargers are actually working and which are frequently occupied.

The company recently introduced a "Reliability Score" for each public charger based on user check-ins, creating accountability for network operators to maintain their equipment — a significant step forward for the Indian market.

Volttic — The Budget Option

Volttic has positioned itself as the most affordable charging network in India, with pricing starting at just ₹10/kWh. Operating primarily in Pune and expanding across Western India, Volttic's 150+ stations use 30 kW and 60 kW DC chargers with a focus on high-traffic urban locations. Their lean operations model — minimal staff, automated billing, and solar-assisted power — allows them to undercut competitors while maintaining reliability.

Partnership Strategies

Across the board, successful charging networks in India follow a common playbook when it comes to partnerships:

  • 1. Fuel station co-location: Tata Power and BP Pulse have agreements with IOCL, HPCL, and BPCL to install chargers at existing petrol pumps — leveraging prime real estate and established customer trust.
  • 2. OEM tie-ups: Ather Grid is exclusive to Ather vehicles (though open to others), ChargeZone has an MG partnership, and Tata Power bundles credits with Tata EVs. These captive ecosystems drive loyalty.
  • 3. Retail & hospitality: Malls, hotels, and restaurant chains are increasingly hosting chargers as a value-add for customers, reducing network operators' capital expenditure.
  • 4. Government tenders: State nodal agencies like EESL issue large tenders for public charging infrastructure, which companies like Tata Power and Fortum have consistently won.
  • 5. Interoperability agreements: More networks are signing roaming agreements, allowing a single RFID card or app to work across multiple networks — reducing fragmentation.

The Road Ahead

India needs an estimated 4,00,000 public charging stations by 2030 to support the projected 10 million EVs on the road. Current installations are growing at 70% CAGR, but significant gaps remain in rural areas, tier-3 cities, and along secondary highways. The government's PM E-DRIVE scheme has allocated ₹2,000 crore specifically for public charging infrastructure, which is expected to accelerate deployment significantly in the next 24 months.

Conclusion

India's EV charging network has evolved from a fragmented patchwork of a few hundred chargers to a rapidly maturing ecosystem of over 25,000 public points operated by a diverse mix of players. Tata Power leads in scale, ChargeZone in highway coverage, Statiq in aggregation, Ather Grid in two-wheeler charging, and regional players like Zeon and Volttic drive affordability.

The key challenges that remain: standardisation across connectors and payment systems, improving charger uptime and maintenance, expanding into underserved regions, and ensuring grid capacity keeps pace with demand. But the trajectory is clear — India's EV charging network is being built at a pace that rivals any major market globally.

For EV buyers, the message is encouraging: range anxiety is becoming less justified by the month. Before purchasing, check which networks have the best coverage in your city and along your regular travel routes — the answer may influence your choice of EV brand.
