Government Policies

A complete breakdown of India's EV policy landscape — central subsidies, state incentives, tax benefits, and what to expect from FAME III.

7 MIN READ POLICY & REGULATIONS

Government policy has been the single biggest driver of India's electric vehicle transition. From the ambitious FAME India scheme to state-level purchase incentives and tax breaks, policymakers have deployed a multi-pronged approach to make EVs affordable and accessible. But with multiple schemes across central and state governments, the policy landscape can be overwhelming for buyers. This guide breaks down every major policy affecting EV adoption in India as of 2026.

01

FAME I — 2015 to 2019

The first phase of Faster Adoption and Manufacturing of Electric Vehicles allocated Rs 895 crore to subsidise 2W, 3W, 4W, and buses. It supported ~2.8 lakh EVs but was criticised for limited impact due to low subsidy amounts and slow disbursement. Focused primarily on technology demonstration and pilot projects.

FAME II — 2019 to 2024

A substantially expanded Rs 10,000 crore outlay with clear focus on public transportation and charging infrastructure. Subsidised 1.6 million EVs, including 1.3 million two-wheelers. Key feature: demand incentives linked to battery capacity (Rs 10,000-15,000 per kWh for 4Ws). Phase extended by 6 months due to unspent funds.

FAME II Extension — 2024 to 2025

Government extended FAME II with an additional Rs 1,500 crore to bridge the gap until FAME III launch. Revised subsidy structure: 4W passenger EVs now receive Rs 5,000 per kWh (capped at Rs 50,000 per vehicle). Focus shifted to ensuring proper verification and preventing subsidy misuse by OEMs.

FAME III — Expected 2026-2030

Expected to launch with an outlay of Rs 15,000-20,000 crore. Anticipated changes: higher subsidies for 4W private cars, dedicated allocation for charging infrastructure (Rs 5,000+ crore), focus on electric trucks and heavy commercial vehicles, and stricter localisation norms to boost domestic manufacturing. Final cabinet approval pending.

The GST Council slashed EV GST from 12% to 5% in 2019, making it one of the most impactful policy moves for EV adoption. Combined with the income tax deduction of up to Rs 1.5 lakh on EV loan interest under Section 80EEB (available until March 2027), the tax savings on an EV purchase can exceed Rs 2-3 lakh compared to an equivalent ICE vehicle.

Additionally, most states have completely waived road tax and registration fees for EVs — saving buyers an additional Rs 20,000-1,00,000 depending on the vehicle price and state. These waivers have been a significant factor in driving EV adoption, particularly in Delhi, Maharashtra, and Gujarat.

03

PLI Scheme for Battery Manufacturing

The Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme for Advanced Chemistry Cell (ACC) Battery Manufacturing, announced in 2021 with an outlay of Rs 18,100 crore, aims to build 50 GWh of domestic battery manufacturing capacity. This is critical for reducing India's dependence on battery imports (currently ~80% of cells are imported from China) and bringing down EV costs.

PLI Scheme Highlights

  • →Rs 18,100 crore outlay over 7 years
  • →50 GWh domestic battery manufacturing target
  • →Incentives of Rs 200-450 per kWh for batteries sold locally
  • →Reliance, Ola, Rajesh Exports, Amara Raja among the approved beneficiaries
  • →Expected to reduce battery pack costs by 25-30% by 2028

05

Deep Dive: Key State Policies

Delhi EV Policy 2.0

Widely regarded as India's most successful state EV policy. Purchase subsidy of Rs 10,000 for 4Ws and up to Rs 30,000 for 2Ws. Combined with road tax and registration waivers, Delhi EV buyers save Rs 30,000-1,50,000 per vehicle. Also mandates that all new shared fleet vehicles be 100% EV by 2028. Over 25% of all new vehicle registrations in Delhi are now EVs.

Maharashtra EV Policy 2021

Offers up to Rs 1.5 lakh subsidy for 4Ws based on battery capacity (Rs 5,000 per kWh). Additional Rs 5,000 per kWh for vehicles with swappable batteries. Targets 10 lakh EVs on road by 2027 and mandates EV charging stations at all new petrol pumps. Mumbai has the highest density of EV showrooms in India.

Gujarat EV Policy 2022

Among the most generous — Rs 10,000 per kWh (up to Rs 1.5 lakh) for 4Ws. Targets 2 lakh EV registrations by 2027. Mandates EV-ready infrastructure in all new residential and commercial buildings. Strong focus on developing the Ahmedabad-Vadodara-Surat charging corridor for inter-city travel.

Tamil Nadu EV Policy 2023

Rs 6,000 per kWh subsidy with a cap of Rs 1 lakh for 4Ws. Aggressive targets for attracting EV manufacturing investments — TN already hosts Ola Electric, Ather Energy, and Hyundai's EV plant in Chennai. Offers additional capital subsidies for OEMs setting up manufacturing units in the state.

Karnataka EV Policy 2022

Up to Rs 1.4 lakh subsidy for 4Ws based on battery capacity and ex-showroom price. Bengaluru leads India in EV startup ecosystem. Policy focuses heavily on B2B and commercial EV adoption, with mandates for e-commerce and delivery fleets to go electric by 2027.

06

What to Expect from FAME III

Industry insiders and policy analysts expect FAME III to be a significantly more ambitious scheme, addressing the gaps identified in FAME II:

  • •Higher outlay: Projected Rs 15,000-20,000 crore, nearly double of FAME II, reflecting the government's enhanced commitment to EV adoption.
  • •4W focus: Increased subsidies for private 4W passenger EVs — rumoured to be Rs 7,500-10,000 per kWh with a higher cap of Rs 1-1.5 lakh per vehicle.
  • •Charging infrastructure fund: A dedicated Rs 5,000+ crore allocation for public charging stations, battery swapping networks, and grid upgrades.
  • •Electric truck incentives: First-time subsidies for heavy commercial EVs to decarbonise India's logistics sector, which accounts for ~12% of transport emissions.
  • •Stricter localisation: Phased manufacturing programme requiring higher domestic value addition to qualify for subsidies, promoting the "Make in India" vision for EVs.
  • •Scrappage linked incentive: Additional subsidy for EV buyers who scrap their old ICE vehicle, creating a replacement cycle that accelerates both EV adoption and end-of-life vehicle management.
KEY TAKEAWAYS FOR BUYERS
  • →An EV buyer in Delhi, Gujarat, or Maharashtra can save Rs 2-4 lakh on a Rs 15-20 lakh car (central subsidy + state subsidy + road tax waiver + GST advantage).
  • →FAME II subsidies are applicable at the point of purchase — the dealer deducts the amount from the ex-showroom price. You do not need to file separately.
  • →State subsidies in Maharashtra, Gujarat, and Karnataka are in addition to FAME II, but some states require separate applications through their EV portals.
  • →Check your state's EV policy website before purchasing — some states have price caps (e.g., only EVs under Rs 15-20 lakh qualify for subsidies).
  • →Income tax benefit under Section 80EEB is available only for loans taken from banks or NBFCs — not for cash purchases. Keep your loan documents ready.
  • →FAME III is expected within 2026 — if you can wait, the subsidies are likely to increase, especially for 4W passenger EVs.
07

Conclusion

India's EV policy framework is among the most comprehensive in the developing world. The combination of central purchase subsidies, GST reductions, income tax benefits, and aggressive state-level incentives has successfully kickstarted the EV market — four-wheeler EV sales grew at a CAGR of over 80% between 2021 and 2025.

However, challenges remain. Subsidy disbursement delays, policy uncertainty around FAME III timelines, and the sunset of existing state policies create a complex landscape for buyers. The government's approach is shifting from direct purchase subsidies toward enabling infrastructure and manufacturing competitiveness — a transition that will define the next phase of India's EV story.

For the prospective EV buyer in 2026, the message is clear: the financial incentives have never been better. With central and state governments offering combined benefits that can reduce the effective cost of an EV below that of a comparable petrol car, the economic case for going electric has never been stronger.
