Renewable Energy and EVs

Discover the powerful synergy between renewable energy and electric vehicles — and how this duo is driving India toward a net-zero future.

7 min read EV Car Wale Team Updated July 2026

The transition to electric mobility is incomplete without a parallel shift to renewable energy. An EV charged by coal-fired power still produces emissions — but an EV charged by the sun or wind is genuinely zero-emission. This article explores the deepening relationship between renewable energy and electric vehicles, with a focus on India's ambitious targets and the technologies making it all possible.

The Clean Energy Loop

🌱 The Clean Energy Loop

Charging EVs with solar or wind power forms a closed, zero-emission energy cycle. EVs can also act as grid stabilizers, storing excess power and returning it to the grid when needed.

Electric vehicles and renewable energy share a mutually reinforcing relationship. Solar and wind farms generate clean electricity; EVs store and use that electricity for transport. When EV batteries are charged from renewable sources, the lifecycle emissions of an electric vehicle drop by up to 70–80% compared to a conventional petrol or diesel car.

This clean energy loop is at the heart of every net-zero strategy worldwide. In India, where the grid still relies heavily on coal (roughly 70% of generation), maximising renewable charging is the single most impactful step an EV owner can take to reduce their carbon footprint.

The graph is simple: more renewables on the grid means cleaner EVs. And more EVs on the road creates a larger market for renewable energy deployment — a virtuous cycle that accelerates the entire energy transition.

Solar EV Charging

Solar-powered EV charging is the most accessible form of renewable charging for Indian homeowners. With over 300 sunny days a year in most parts of the country, India is ideally positioned to adopt rooftop solar for EV charging.

A typical 5 kW rooftop solar system can generate 20–25 units per day — enough to charge a mid-size EV (like the Tata Nexon EV with a 30 kWh battery) from 20% to 100% in about 5–6 hours of peak sunlight. The excess power feeds back into the grid through net metering, earning credits on your electricity bill.

The government's PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana (subsidising rooftop solar for 1 crore households) is expected to accelerate adoption, making solar + EV a financially compelling combination for Indian families.

Wind-Powered Charging Stations

While solar dominates the residential segment, wind energy is increasingly powering commercial EV charging stations, especially in states like Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, and Maharashtra — India's wind energy hubs.

Companies like Tata Power and ChargeZone have begun setting up charging stations directly connected to wind farms, ensuring that every kilowatt-hour dispensed comes from a renewable source. These stations are typically located along major highways, enabling genuinely green long-distance travel.

Wind power has the advantage of generating electricity at night (when solar is unavailable), complementing solar to provide round-the-clock renewable charging. Hybrid solar-wind charging hubs are becoming increasingly common in progressive markets.

Vehicle-to-Grid (V2G) Technology

V2G technology turns EVs into mobile energy storage units. Instead of only drawing power from the grid, a V2G-enabled EV can discharge electricity back into the grid during peak demand hours — and get paid for it.

This concept is particularly powerful in India, where peak demand often exceeds supply by 10–15 GW. A fleet of 1 million EVs with V2G capability could theoretically provide 5–10 GW of grid support — enough to eliminate most blackouts during peak hours.

💰

Revenue for Owners

EV owners can earn ₹5,000–12,000 per year by selling power back to the grid during peak hours, offsetting charging costs significantly.

⚡

Grid Stabilisation

Thousands of parked EV batteries can collectively act as a virtual power plant, smoothing out renewable energy intermittency and reducing coal plant reliance.

🌿

Maximised Renewables

Excess solar/wind energy generated during off-peak hours can be stored in EV batteries and released when renewables are scarce — eliminating curtailment.

Did you know? The first V2G pilot in India was launched in 2023 by Indraprastha Institute of Information Technology (IIIT) Delhi in partnership with Tata Power. The project demonstrated that a fleet of 10 electric SUVs could supply 50 kW of power back to the campus grid during peak hours — enough to power 20–30 households.

Green Hydrogen vs Battery EVs

Green hydrogen — produced by splitting water using renewable electricity — is often presented as an alternative to battery-electric vehicles. But the two technologies are more complementary than competitive.

Battery EVs are more energy-efficient (75–85% well-to-wheel efficiency) and are ideal for passenger cars, two-wheelers, and short-haul commercial vehicles. Green hydrogen fuel cell EVs (FCEVs) are better suited for heavy-duty trucking, shipping, and industrial applications where battery weight and charging time are limiting factors.

India's National Green Hydrogen Mission (2023) targets 5 million tonnes of green hydrogen production annually by 2030, with a focus on industrial decarbonisation. For mainstream personal transport, battery EVs remain the clear winner — but both technologies will play a role in India's net-zero journey.

India's 500 GW Renewable Target

India has committed to installing 500 GW of renewable energy capacity by 2030 — a 2.5x increase from today's ~200 GW. This target includes 280 GW of solar, 140 GW of wind, and the remainder from hydro, biomass, and emerging technologies.

For EV owners, this means the grid will get significantly cleaner over the next five years. By 2030, the average grid emission factor in India is projected to drop from ~0.7 kg CO₂/kWh to under 0.4 kg CO₂/kWh — making every EV on Indian roads about 40% cleaner than it is today.

The synergy works both ways: mass EV adoption creates additional electricity demand that incentivises further renewable capacity deployment. The Ministry of Power estimates that 100 million EVs on Indian roads by 2030 would require ~80 GW of additional generation — all of which can be met through planned renewable additions.

Corporate Adoption of Green Fleets

Indian corporations are increasingly committing to electric fleets powered by renewable energy. Companies like Zomato, Swiggy, Flipkart, Amazon, and Tata Group have announced ambitious EV integration targets for their delivery and logistics operations, paired with on-site solar installations.

Zomato, for example, has committed to 100% EV deliveries by 2030 and is installing solar canopies at its delivery hubs to charge its fleet of electric two-wheelers. Flipkart has deployed over 1,000 electric three-wheelers across 25 cities, with plans to scale to 25,000 by 2027 — all charged from warehouses powered by rooftop solar.

This corporate green fleet trend is creating a powerful market signal: clean logistics is not just an environmental choice but a cost-saving one, with fleet operators reporting 30–50% lower running costs compared to internal combustion fleets.

The Road to Net Zero

India has set a target of achieving net-zero emissions by 2070. Transport currently accounts for ~14% of India's energy-related CO₂ emissions, and electrification paired with renewable energy is the most viable pathway to decarbonise this sector.

The key milestones on this journey include:

  • 2030 500 GW renewable capacity; 30% EV penetration for new vehicle sales; 50% reduction in grid emission intensity.
  • 2040 80% EV sales penetration; coal phased down to under 30% of generation; green hydrogen commercially viable for heavy transport.
  • 2050 Near-100% renewable grid; full electrification of light transport; V2G widely adopted as standard technology.
  • 2070 Net-zero emissions achieved; India becomes a global clean energy leader with a fully sustainable transport ecosystem.

Conclusion

The relationship between renewable energy and electric vehicles is one of the most powerful dynamics in the global energy transition. In India, where both solar potential and EV adoption are growing at extraordinary rates, the synergy is particularly promising.

For the individual EV owner, the message is clear: pairing your EV with a rooftop solar system or choosing a charging provider that uses renewable energy dramatically reduces your carbon footprint and saves you money over time. For the nation, every EV on the road is another step toward energy independence and a cleaner environment.

As India accelerates toward its 500 GW renewable target and 30% EV penetration by 2030, the clean energy loop will only grow stronger — proving that renewables and EVs are not just complementary, but essential partners in the fight against climate change.

💡 Key Takeaways: Renewable Synergy
  • ✔ Zero Emission Cycle: Clean generation + EV storage maximizes the carbon reduction benefits of electric vehicles.
  • ✔ V2G (Vehicle-to-Grid): Future-proofing the grid by allowing EVs to discharge power back during peak demand hours.
  • ✔ Storage Solution: EV batteries help solve the intermittent nature of solar and wind generation.
